# Product site research (5 Nov 2026)

Pages read: Zed, Raycast, Warp, Obsidian, Conductor, cmux, Linear, Arc (now a Dia farewell page). Ghostty's page didn't come through the fetcher, so its notes are from memory. superset.sh now redirects to paradev.com.

- **Headlines are short.** Zed "Your last next editor", Raycast "Your shortcut to everything", Obsidian "Sharpen your thinking". Four words or so, then one plain line under it that says what the thing is.
- **Two actions in the hero, no more.** Zed: Download + Clone source. cmux: Download for Mac + View on GitHub. For an open-source app, "download" plus "source" is the norm.
- **Download is one main button.** Obsidian labels it with your OS ("Download for Windows") and adds a quiet "More platforms" link. Nobody stacks four big buttons in the hero. Zed puts a single line under the button saying which platforms it runs on.
- **The download sits in three places:** the nav, the hero and the closing section (cmux does exactly this). People who scroll to the end shouldn't have to scroll back up.
- **They show the product itself, not an illustration.** Linear's hero is a real-looking issue view. Zed shows its own UI. Warp animates a status grid (queued / working / waiting / shipped), which is close to our tile wall idea and proves a live mock reads better than a static shot.
- **Ghostty's hero is the product's own medium** (an animated terminal). A live HTML mock of our tile wall does the same job for us: it is the app's real behaviour, not a video.
- **Features alternate text and one big visual.** Linear and Zed: H2, two lines, one panel. The best pages give each feature one picture; walls of six icon cards feel generic.
- **Big companies use logos and testimonials; small open-source tools don't need them.** cmux and Conductor are short and plain. We have no users yet, so we skip social proof entirely rather than invent it.
- **Privacy is said in three short points.** Obsidian: stored on your device, works offline, open formats. Our version: no account, no telemetry, nothing leaves your machine.
- **"Free" is stated once, flatly.** Obsidian: "Free without limits". No pricing table needed when there is no price.
- **FAQ near the end** (cmux ~16 questions, Warp ~10, Arc). It catches the practical worries: platforms, cost, how it relates to the thing it wraps. Ours: is it the real CLI, does it cost tokens, why the security warning.
- **Docs are one link in the nav plus "read the docs" at the end.** cmux's docs are task-shaped: getting started, shortcuts, configuration, notifications.
- **Unsigned-build notes live with the download**, not buried. cmux and Conductor are notarised so they don't need it; we do, so the install steps go right under the download section and in the docs.
- **What to avoid** (seen on the busier pages): announcement pills, numbered "Fig 0.1" rows (Linear), logo strips, case-study numbers, "trusted by" lines. They read as marketing, and the owner's rules ban most of them anyway.
- **Positioning gap:** Conductor ("parallel agents on your Mac") and cmux ("terminal built for multitasking") sell worktrees, tabs and throughput. Nobody sells calm. Our page should feel slower and quieter than theirs, and say Mac and Windows early, since most rivals are Mac only.
