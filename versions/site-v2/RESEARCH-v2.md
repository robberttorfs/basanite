# Site v2 research (5 Nov 2026)

Fetched this time: Tuple, Zed, Teenage Engineering (OP-1 page). Ghostty's page still doesn't come through the fetcher. Linear, Raycast, Warp, Vercel Ship and Rauno's and Paco's sites are from memory of their pages, so treat those notes as "how they felt", not quotes.

Moves worth stealing, and what we take from each:

- **Ghostty: the hero is the product's own medium.** The page opens on a live terminal, not a picture of one. We do the same with the tile wall, and go one step further: the headline lives inside a tile, so the page is a session too.
- **Tuple: copy that knows it's marketing.** Eyebrows like "Final, desperate CTA" and "Corporate-approved fun". One or two jokes land; a whole page of them turns smug. Take the dry tone for headings, keep body copy plain and useful.
- **Teenage Engineering: specs set as plain label and value, short declarative lines.** "if it feels thinner. it is." Their spec lists are typeset like a manual, not a feature grid. We use that for the "everything else" list: set like `--help` output, which is our product's native manual format.
- **Linear: one accent, used sparingly, on near-black.** Everything is grey until something matters. Ours is stricter: amber appears only where something needs you, in the demo and in the screenshots. The download button is not amber.
- **Raycast: the ⌘K palette as a page feature.** Their site leans on the keyboard. A small palette on our page (⌘K or Ctrl+K, plus a keycap button for touch) is the second live piece of the product, and it doubles as navigation and download.
- **Warp: a status grid as a live mock.** Queued, working, waiting, shipped. Proves a moving mock reads faster than a screenshot. We keep it slower than theirs: work happens every couple of seconds, not in a blur.
- **Zed: one plain line under a short headline, and the product right away.** No paragraph of preamble before the UI.
- **Vercel Ship pages: type scale as the drama.** Huge tight headlines, almost nothing else. Contrast from size and weight, not from gradients.
- **Rauno and Paco: small motion done exactly.** Short, eased, interruptible; nothing bounces; layout never jumps under your cursor. That is our product's own rule (the tile you're in never moves), so the page has to obey it as well.

What v1 did that reads as template, and what changes:

- Big black system-sans headline, grey body, centred hero, then a three-column "Calm / No extra tokens / Mac and Windows" row. Generic shapes. v2 drops the three-column rows entirely.
- The hero wall sat under the copy as a demo. v2 makes the wall the hero and puts the copy in it.
- System fonts everywhere: safe, but no voice. v2 uses one characterful grotesk with a width axis for headings and a real mono for everything that comes from a terminal.
- Slate-blue greys and three status colours (blue, green, amber). v2 is carbon, bone and amber: working and done are shapes and words in neutral tones, so amber is the only colour on the page.
