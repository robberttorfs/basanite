# Site v5 research: what to write and what to show (6 Nov 2026)

Owner on v4: great potential, but the name is too big, keep only the first screen in that style, make the rest regular scrolling with smooth scroll, less AI text, more product. "Every Claude Code session on one calm screen" reads as typical AI.

## How real developer tools write
Fetched this round: Zed, Conductor, cmux, Paradev (ex Superset), Warp, Obsidian, Sublime Text. Ghostty didn't come through.

| Site | Main line | Download |
|---|---|---|
| Conductor | "Run parallel coding agents on your Mac." | |
| cmux | "The terminal built for multitasking, organization, and programmability." | "Download for Mac", "View on GitHub" beside it |
| Zed | "The world's fastest IDE. Open source. AI optional." | "Download" |
| Sublime Text | "Text Editing, Done Right" | "Download for Windows" |
| Obsidian | "Sharpen your thinking." | "Get Obsidian for Windows", "More platforms" beside it |
| Paradev | "Run an Army of Coding Agents" | "Download for macOS" |

Takeaways:
1. **Say what it does with a verb and the thing's name.** Conductor's line is the model: no mood words. Ours: "Run all your Claude Code sessions in one window."
2. **Section headings are feature names.** Obsidian: "Links." "Graph." "Canvas." cmux: "Vertical tabs", "Notification rings". So: Tiles, Overview, Usage, Workspaces. Then one or two sentences of plain fact.
3. **The product is the picture.** cmux and Paradev show the app straight after the hero. So the first thing under the stone is the real app, full width.
4. **Download names the OS, and GitHub sits right beside it.** Every open-source one does this. "Other platforms" is one quiet link.
5. **Long tail as a plain list.** cmux lists ten features as bold label plus one line. That replaces more big sections.
6. Avoid: mood words (calm, effortless), "not X, just Y", three-beat slogans, rhetorical sections like "Smash your backlog", and logos or testimonials we don't have.

## What to show, and in what order
From docs/product/research/02-user-wants.md (how often a pain came up) and 01-competitors.md (what only we do):
1. **Tiles.** "Which session needs me" is the biggest theme, and nobody else sizes tiles by need.
2. **Overview.** Same pain, one step further: answering from a list.
3. **Usage.** Seen about 7 times, and limits always on screen is rare.
4. **Workspaces and palette.** Organising many sessions (seen 4 to 6 times).
5. **Changes.** The review bottleneck.
6. **Modes, then the long tail.** Real guards are unique, but they take a sentence to get.
7. **Four facts.** People distrust wrappers and token waste, so: no extra tokens, no telemetry, your settings, MIT.
8. **Download, questions.**

## Effects kept below the first screen
Smooth scroll, screenshots that rise in and bend with scroll speed and lean towards the cursor, the wall dimming except the waiting tile, hover rows. No pinning and no scroll-driven scenes.
