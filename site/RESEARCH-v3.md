# Site v3 research (5 Nov 2026)

Brief: simple but striking. One idea, one visual, a confident download. v2 was "too much going on", and its hero was a fake window full of terminal text.

Fetched this time: Raycast, Linear, Things 3, Notion Calendar, Superhuman, Warp, Apple AirPods Pro, Flexoki. Ghostty, cmux and Rosé Pine's palette page didn't come through the fetcher. Arc, Craft, Pitch, Teenage Engineering, Rauno's and Mitchell's sites are from memory, so those lines say how the pages felt, not what they say word for word. The owner's own portfolio research (FINDINGS, notes 02/04/07/08, V4-FEEDBACK) is folded in where it applies.

## Takeaways

1. **The headline names the thing; the visual does the explaining.** Things opens on one word, "Things", with one sentence. Notion Calendar says "It's time." and lets the video work. Nobody explains the mechanism in the hero copy, so ours shouldn't either: the moving picture shows the glow.
2. **Hero visuals that read in 3 seconds are diagrams, not screenshots.** Warp's hero is a grid of block characters with a four-word legend (queued, working, waiting, shipped). It is the closest thing to our idea, and it works because there is no text inside the cells. Our tiles carry one word each at most.
3. **Real screenshots come second, as proof.** Linear, Notion Calendar and Superhuman put the real UI right under the promise. The abstract hero says what it does; the next screen says "and here it is, for real". That order fixes v2, which tried to make the hero do both.
4. **One idea per screen, two to five words per headline.** Apple's AirPods page: "All-day battery life", "Audio performance", "Fitness". Notion Calendar alternates feature rows with full-width banner headlines that have no body text at all. Headlines that short let the type be huge.
5. **The hero has one main action.** Superhuman: one button. Notion Calendar: one main button plus "Download for macOS". For an open-source desktop app that becomes: one button naming your OS, and one quiet line of the other builds plus the source.
6. **Raycast leads with the keyboard as the visual.** A whole page built around one object people already know. Ours is the tile wall, an object people already know from their own terminal splits.
7. **Spotlight dimming (owner's research, the "perfume" pattern): everything starts dim and the one item that matters lights up.** That is literally what the app does. It is the hero motion, and it is also how the real screenshot gets shown: dim the whole window, keep the waiting tile at full brightness.
8. **Teenage Engineering and Things: confidence comes from emptiness.** Wide margins, few elements, product dead centre, short declarative lines. Professional, not playful, which is the direction the owner asked for after his "too childish" portfolio.
9. **Linear and Raycast keep colour off the page until it means something.** Near-black, greys, one accent. Our signal is stricter: amber appears only where something needs you, and the download button is not amber.
10. **Neutrals tinted towards a hue look designed; slate looks default** (owner's note 08, verified on landonorris.com and trevornoah.com). The tint is where a palette gets its character, so the signal can stay single.
11. **Terminal and editor themes people love are low-saturation bases with a few warm, clear accents:** Kanagawa (ink `#1F1F28`, carp yellow `#E6C384`), Gruvbox (`#282828` with `#FABD2F`), Rosé Pine (`#191724` with gold `#F6C177`), Vesper (`#101010` with peach `#FFC799`), Flexoki (ink on paper, accents tuned in Oklab). Warm yellow on a cool dark base is the developer-loved combination, and it is the app's own amber.
12. **Rauno and Mitchell: small motion, done exactly.** Short eased transitions, nothing bouncing, nothing moving under the cursor. The page should obey the app's rule: the thing you're in never moves.
13. **Superhuman and Raycast show what to avoid:** a scroll of nine product sections, glass cards on gradient backdrops, avatar walls. Our page stops at about five sections.
14. **Download lives in three places** (from v1 research, still true): nav, hero, end. Unsigned first-launch steps sit next to the end download, not in the hero.
15. **Owner taste from the portfolio rounds:** likes big clear letters and one clear path down the page, likes blue with restraint, dislikes red and orange as the main colour and yellow used everywhere, dislikes hard-edged colour blocks between sections. So: amber stays a small signal, never a block; blue lives in the neutrals; one continuous background.
